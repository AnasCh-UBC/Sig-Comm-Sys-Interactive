01 What is modulation?

Data transmission

▤Analog dataSpeech, music, video
⇄Sampling + conversionAnalog to digital
▦Digital dataText, files, digital media
↗CommunicationAnalog or digital modulation

Modulation impresses a signal on the amplitude, phase, or frequency of a carrier.

Lowpass versus bandpass signals

Lowpass (baseband) signal

A signal with frequency components around \(f=0\), and no components beyond some bandwidth \(W\).

Bandpass signal

A signal whose spectrum occupies a band away from \(f=0\), usually centered around a carrier frequency \(f_c\).

Communication channels and regulations specify the frequency bands available for transmission. Modulation moves a lowpass signal to the designated band.

Analog modulation

Consider a message signal \(m(t)\), typically a lowpass signal with bandwidth \(W\) Hz, and a high-frequency carrier \(c(t)=\cos(2\pi f_ct)\).

\[ x(t)=A(t)\cos\big(2\pi f_ct+\phi(t)\big). \]
  • Amplitude modulation (AM): the instantaneous amplitude \(A(t)\) is a function of \(m(t)\).
  • Angle modulation: the phase \(\phi(t)\), or its derivative, is a function of \(m(t)\).

Objectives of modulation

Modulation converts the lowpass message \(m(t)\) to a bandpass signal around \(f_c\), in order to:

  • Match the transmitted spectrum to the passband characteristics of the communication channel.
  • Simplify transmitter structure by employing a high frequency, which allows smaller antennas.
  • Accommodate multiple transmissions using different carrier frequencies.
  • Expand the transmitted bandwidth to increase noise and interference immunity.
Amplitude modulation: the message signal is impressed on the amplitude of the carrier. This lecture focuses on two forms: double-sideband suppressed-carrier (DSB-SC) AM and conventional double-sideband (DSB) AM.

02 Double-sideband suppressed-carrier (DSB-SC) AM

DSB-SC modulation

A DSB-SC signal is the product of the message and carrier:

\[ x(t)=A_c\,m(t)\cos(2\pi f_ct). \]

Here, \(A_c\cos(2\pi f_ct)\) is the carrier, with amplitude \(A_c\) and frequency \(f_c\).

The spectrum has sidebands at \(f=\pm(f_c-f_m)\) and \(f=\pm(f_c+f_m)\), with no carrier impulses at \(f=\pm f_c\).

Spectrum of DSB-SC AM

Using the Fourier-transform modulation property:

\[ X(f)=\mathcal{F}\{x(t)\} =\frac{A_c}{2}\left[M(f-f_c)+M(f+f_c)\right], \] where \(M(f)=\mathcal{F}\{m(t)\}\).

If the message bandwidth is \(W\), the modulated signal occupies two sidebands and has total bandwidth \(2W\).

Upper sideband (USB)

Frequency components above the positive carrier frequency, \(|f|>f_c\).

It is called double-sideband because both sidebands are present, and suppressed-carrier because there is no carrier impulse at \(\pm f_c\).

Single-tone message example

For \(m(t)=a\cos(2\pi f_mt)\), with \(f_m\ll f_c\), use the product-to-sum identity:

\[ \begin{aligned} x(t)&=aA_c\cos(2\pi f_mt)\cos(2\pi f_ct)\\ &=\frac{aA_c}{2}\left[ \cos(2\pi(f_c-f_m)t)+ \cos(2\pi(f_c+f_m)t)\right]. \end{aligned} \]

The spectrum consists of impulses at the two sideband frequencies around each of \(+f_c\) and \(-f_c\). For a sine message, the same four frequencies appear, with the corresponding phase signs.

Power of DSB-SC AM \[ P=\frac{A_c^2}{2}P_m, \] where \(P_m\) is the average power of the message signal. For \(m(t)=a\cos(2\pi f_mt)\), \(P_m=a^2/2\), so \[ P=\frac{A_c^2a^2}{4}. \]

Half of this power is in the USB and the other half is in the LSB.

Demodulation and phase synchronization

For \(r(t)=x(t)=A_cm(t)\cos(2\pi f_ct)\), the receiver multiplies by \(\cos(2\pi f_ct+\phi)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} y(t)&=r(t)\cos(2\pi f_ct+\phi)\\ &=\frac{A_c}{2}m(t)\cos\phi +\frac{A_c}{2}m(t)\cos(4\pi f_ct+\phi). \end{aligned} \]

The lowpass filter removes the high-frequency term, leaving

\[ \hat m(t)=\frac{A_c}{2}m(t)\cos\phi. \]
Why synchronization matters: if \(\phi=\pi/2\), the recovered signal vanishes. At \(\phi=\pi/4\), its power is reduced by a factor of \(\cos^2(\phi)=1/2\). A coherent or synchronous demodulator aims for \(\phi=0\).

Adding a pilot tone

One way to help the receiver synchronize its local oscillator is to add a small pilot tone at the carrier frequency to the transmitted signal:

\[ x_{\mathrm{pilot}}(t)=A_cm(t)\cos(2\pi f_ct)+A_p\cos(2\pi f_ct). \]

At the receiver, a narrowband bandpass filter centered at \(f_c\) extracts the pilot tone for use as the local oscillator. The disadvantage is that some transmitted power is allocated to the pilot. A phase-locked loop (PLL) can provide an alternative carrier-recovery method.

03 Conventional AM (DSB AM)

From DSB-SC to conventional AM

In DSB-SC, the message \(m(t)\) and its negative trace the envelope of the modulated waveform. By biasing (shifting) the message before modulation, the envelope can remain nonnegative, allowing the receiver to recover the message using an envelope detector.

Conventional AM signal \[ x(t)=A_c[1+a\,m_n(t)]\cos(2\pi f_ct), \] where \[ m_n(t)=\frac{m(t)}{\max_t|m(t)|},\qquad |m_n(t)|\leq1, \] and \(a\in(0,1)\) is the modulation index. The normalization and \(a<1\) ensure that \(A_c[1+a\,m_n(t)]\geq0\), which allows envelope detection.

Power of conventional AM

Assuming the normalized message has zero average, the total power is

\[ P=\frac{A_c^2}{2}a^2P_{m_n}+\frac{A_c^2}{2}, \] where \(P_{m_n}\) is the average power of \(m_n(t)\). The first term is the information-bearing sideband power, while the second term is the carrier power.
Information-bearing power\[ P_{\mathrm{SB}}=\frac{A_c^2a^2}{2}P_{m_n} \]
Carrier power\[ P_{\mathrm{carrier}}=\frac{A_c^2}{2} \]

Usually, the information-bearing power is smaller than the carrier power. Conventional AM therefore uses power less efficiently than DSB-SC AM.

Example: power of a two-tone message

Consider \(m(t)=p\cos(2\pi f_1t)+q\sin(2\pi f_2t)\), with \(f_2=3f_1\), \(p=3\), \(q=1\), \(\max_t|m(t)|=3.7\), and modulation index \(a=0.85\).

The normalized message is

\[ m_n(t)=u\cos(2\pi f_1t)+v\sin(2\pi f_2t), \quad u=0.81,\quad v=0.27. \]

Its average power is

\[ P_{m_n}=\frac{u^2}{2}+\frac{v^2}{2}=0.36. \]

For \(A_c=1\), the information-bearing power is approximately \(0.13\), and the carrier power is \(0.5\).

04 Implementation of modulation and demodulation

Why a nonlinear device?

Modulation generates new frequency components and therefore cannot be modeled as an LTI system. A nonlinear device, such as a diode, can generate the required modulated components.

Power-law modulation

A power-law modulator adds the message and carrier, passes their sum through a nonlinear device, and then uses a bandpass filter centered at \(f_c\):

\[ v_i(t)=m(t)+A_c\cos(2\pi f_ct), \qquad v_o(t)=a_1v_i(t)+a_2v_i^2(t). \]

The bandpass filter selects the terms around \(f_c\), giving

\[ \begin{aligned} x(t)&=a_1A_c\cos(2\pi f_ct)+2a_2A_cm(t)\cos(2\pi f_ct)\\ &=a_1A_c\left[1+\frac{2a_2}{a_1}m(t)\right]\cos(2\pi f_ct). \end{aligned} \]

If \(\frac{2a_2}{a_1}|m(t)|<1\), the result is a conventional AM signal.

Balanced modulator

A balanced modulator consists of two conventional AM modulators, one driven by \(m(t)\) and the other by \(-m(t)\). Subtracting their outputs cancels the carrier:

\[ \begin{aligned} x(t)&=A_c[1+m(t)]\cos(2\pi f_ct)\\ &\quad-A_c[1-m(t)]\cos(2\pi f_ct)\\ &=2A_cm(t)\cos(2\pi f_ct). \end{aligned} \]

The result is a DSB-SC signal.

Envelope detector circuit

An envelope detector uses a diode (rectifier) followed by a parallel \(RC\) network. During positive peaks, the diode conducts and the capacitor charges. When the input falls below the capacitor voltage, the diode becomes reverse-biased and the capacitor discharges through \(R\).

  • During the positive half-cycle, the diode conducts and the capacitor charges.
  • When \(r(t)\) falls below the capacitor voltage, the diode disconnects the input and the capacitor discharges through \(R\).
  • The \(RC\) network smooths the rectified signal and follows its envelope.
Choosing the detector time constant: the envelope detector is useful when \(a<1\) (no overmodulation). The discharge time must be long compared with a carrier period but short compared with the message variation: \[ \frac{1}{f_c}\ll RC\ll\frac{1}{W}. \] Too small an \(RC\) causes fast discharge and ripple. Too large an \(RC\) causes the output to change too slowly and can make it nearly constant.
General DSB-SC demodulator architecture

The receiver multiplies \(r(t)\) by a locally recovered carrier using a balanced modulator, then lowpass filters the result. Carrier recovery is essential:

  • For a suppressed carrier, a phase-locked loop (PLL) can recover the carrier.
  • When a carrier or pilot tone is present, a narrowband bandpass filter around \(f_c\) can extract it.
  • If a large carrier component is transmitted, an envelope detector can be used instead.
