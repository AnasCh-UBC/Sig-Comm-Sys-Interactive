1Fourier series to Fourier transform

From periodic to nonperiodic signals

Periodic signals

Fourier series represents a periodic signal with frequency \(f_0\) as a sum of harmonically related complex sinusoids with frequencies \(k f_0\) with \(k\in\mathbb{Z}\).

General signals (periodic and nonperiodic)

Fourier transform represents a signal as an integral of complex sinusoids with frequencies \(f\in\mathbb{R}\).

The frequency components in a Fourier transform are no longer restricted to discrete harmonics; they form a continuous spectrum.

Analysis: Fourier transform

\[X(f)=\mathcal{F}\{x(t)\}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-j2\pi ft}\,dt.\]

Synthesis: Inverse Fourier transform

\[x(t)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{X(f)\}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}X(f)e^{j2\pi ft}\,df.\]

The Fourier transform produces the frequency domain representation of the signal. The inverse Fourier transform reconstructs the time-domain signal:

Angular-frequency form

Using angular frequency \(\omega=2\pi f\), the transform pair can also be written as

\[X(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt,\qquad x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}X(\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega.\]

In this course, we primarily use frequency \(f\) in Hertz.

What do we plot?

Amplitude spectrum

\(|X(f)|\) versus frequency \(f\). It shows the strength of each frequency component.

Phase spectrum

\(\angle X(f)\) versus frequency \(f\). It shows the phase associated with each component.

The spectrum is generally complex-valued. An amplitude spectrum may also be displayed on a decibel scale as \(10\log_{10}|X(f)|\).

Important observation: A short-duration or impulsive signal generally has a broad frequency spectrum. The extreme case is \(\delta(t)\), whose transform is constant at all frequencies.

The Fourier transform of a signal \(x(t)\) allows us to define the signal's bandwidth.

Bandwidth

The bandwidth \(B\) of a signal \(x(t)\) is the width of the smallest frequency interval \([f_L,f_H]\) outside which \(|X(f)|=0\), i.e., \(B = f_H-f_L.\)
A baseband signal is a signal with \(f_L=0\). Its bandwidth is \(B=f_H\).

In practical systems, spectra may not be strictly zero outside a finite interval. Bandwidth is then defined using an agreed threshold on \(|X(f)|\). In particular, the interval \([f_L,f_H]\) is defined as the interval of frequencies outside which \(|X(f)|\lt \text{threshold}.\)

2Properties of the Fourier transform

Let \(x(t), x_1(t), x_2(t)\) be signals whose Fourier transforms are \(X(f), X_1(f), X_2(f)\), respectively. Then, we have the following properties.

Real-valued signal properties

If \(x(t)\) is real, then \(|X(f)|\) is Hermitian symmetric: \[X(-f)=X^*(f).\]

If \(x(t)\) is real and even, then \(X(f)\) is real and even: \[X(-f)=-X(f).\]

If \(x(t)\) is real and odd, then \(X(f)\) is imaginary and odd: \[X(-f)=-X(f).\]

For real-valued \(x(t)\), it Fourier transform satisfies \(|X(-f)|=|X(f)|\) (even magnitude) and \(\angle X(-f)=-\angle X(f)\) (odd phase) where the phase is defined.

These properties are helpful for validating your calculated Fourier transform.

Examples

The signal in practice 1.1 is real and even, and its spectrum is real and even. The signal in practice 1.3 is real and odd, and its spectrum is purely imaginary and odd.

Time/frequency scaling and shifting

Time shifting

The Fourier transform of a delayed signal \(x(t-t_0)\) is \(X(f)e^{-j2\pi ft_0}\).

Delay in time is phase shift in frequency.

Time scaling

The Fourier transform of \(x(at)\) is \(\frac{1}{|a|}X(\frac{f}{a})\).

Time scaling leads to frequency and amplitude scaling in the frequency domain

Modulation

The Fourier transform of \(x(t) e^{j2\pi f_0t}\) is \(X(f-f_0)\).

Modulating \(x(t)\) (multiplying it by a complex sinusoid) shifts its frequency.

Linearity, duality, and convolution

Linearity

The Fourier transform of \(a_1x_1(t)+a_2x_2(t)\) is \(a_1X_1(f)+a_2X_2(f).\)

Weighted sum in time \(\to\) weigthed sum frequency

Duality

The Fourier transform of \(X(t)\) is \(x(-f)\), and the Fourier transform of \(X(-t)\) is \(x(f)\).

Symmetry relation between the time and frequency domains

Convolution

The Fourier transform of \(x_1(t)\circledast x_2(t)\) is \(X_1(f)X_2(f).\)

Convolution in the time domain is multiplication in the frequency domain

Note: A similar convolution property holds for frequency domain convolution. The Fourier transform of \(x_1(t)x_2(t)\) is \(X_1(f)\circledast X_2(f)\).

Differentiation and integration

Time differentiation

The Fourier transform of \(\frac{d^n x(t)}{dt^n}\) is \((j2\pi f)^nX(f)\).

Differentiation in the time domain is multiplication by \(j2\pi f\) in the frequency domain.

Frequency differentiation

The Fourier transform of \(\left(\frac{2\pi t}{j}\right)^n x(t)\) is \(\frac{d^nX(f)}{df^n}.\)

Differentiation in the frequency domain is multiplication by \(\frac{2\pi t}{j}\) in the time domain.

Integration

The Fourier transform of \(\int_{-\infty}^t x(\tau)d\tau\) is \(\frac{X(f)}{j2\pi f}+\frac{X(0)\delta(f)}{2}.\)

Integration in the time domain is division by \(j2\pi f\) in the frequency domain plus a pulse at \(f=0\).

These properties help in obtaining the Fourier transform of signals that would be more difficult to derive directly, and simplify the analysis of LTI systems.

Parseval's relation

The Fourier transform satisfies \(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x_1(t)x_2^*(t)\,dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}X_1(f)X_2^*(f)\,df.\)

If \(x_1(t)=x_2(t)=x(t)\) we obtain the signal energy \[E_x=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x(t)|^2dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|X(f)|^2df.\]

Energy can be calcuated by integrating the square-magnitued in the time domain or the frequency domain.

Example

The energy of \(x(t)=\text{sinc}^2(t)\) is \(E_x=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{sinc}^4(t)dt\).

Instead of solving this integral, we can use Parseval's relation: Knowing that \(X(f)=\Lambda(f)\), it follows that \(E_x=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Lambda^2(f)df=\frac{2}{3}\).

This integral is easier to solve!

Autocorrelation

The autocorrelation of a signal \(R_x(\tau)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t)x^*(t-\tau)dt\) has a Fourier transform \(\mathcal{F}\{R_x(\tau)\}=|X(f)|^2\) known as the energy spectral density (energy per Hz).

4Fourier transform and LTI systems

Transmission through an LTI system

For an LTI system with impulse response \(h(t)\), input \(x(t)\), and output \(y(t)=h(t)\circledast x(t)\), the convolution property gives

\[Y(f)=H(f)X(f).\]

Frequency-domain analysis often simplifies system analysis: convolution becomes multiplication, and each frequency component is scaled and phase-shifted by \(H(f)\).

Ideal filters

An ideal filter is a system that passes some part of \(X(f)\) and blocks other parts.

Lowpass (LPF)

Passes \(|f|\le W\), rejects frequencies above \(W\).

Highpass (HPF)

Rejects \(|f|\le W\), passes frequencies above \(W\).

Bandpass (BPF)

Passes \(W_1\le|f|\le W_2\), rejects frequencies outside the band.

A filter may have a linear phase term \(e^{-j2\pi ft_d}\) in its passband and still be considered ideal: it delays the signal by \(t_d\).

Nonideal filters

A nonideal filter is a system which scales \(X(f)\) differently depending on \(f\). It attenuates \(X(f)\) more severely outside its passband.

Its 3-dB cutoff frequency is where the power gain is half its maximum, equivalently where the magnitude is \(1/\sqrt2\) of its maximum.

Practial filters are nonideal.
